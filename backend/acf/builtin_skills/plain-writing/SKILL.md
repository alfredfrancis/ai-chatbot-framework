# Plain writing

## Steps

1. Ask for (or find) a sample of the user's own writing if they want it to sound like them. Note their
   habits: sentence length, formality, contractions, how they open and close.
2. Rewrite, keeping every fact and the meaning. Then reread it once only for the patterns below.
3. Return the new text first; list the main changes in a line or two only if asked.

## Patterns to remove

- Puffed-up importance: "plays a pivotal role", "a testament to", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Vague sources: "experts say", "studies show" with no source.
- Sales language: "seamless", "cutting-edge", "robust", "unlock", "elevate", "game-changer".
- Overused words: "delve", "leverage", "navigate", "landscape", "tapestry", "realm", "foster".
- Everything in threes, and "not just X, but Y".
- Endings like "-ing" phrases that add nothing ("..., highlighting the importance of ...").
- Hedging stacks: "it could potentially be argued that".
- Filler: "It's worth noting that", "In conclusion", "I hope this helps", "Great question".
- Too many dashes, bold words, emoji, and headings or bullet lists for what should be a paragraph.
- Title Case Headings when the user writes in sentence case.

## Keep

- Specific details: names, numbers, examples. Replace a general claim with a concrete one when the
  source text has it.
- Varied sentence length, and plain words like "is", "has", "use".
- The user's own voice, even when it breaks a rule above.
